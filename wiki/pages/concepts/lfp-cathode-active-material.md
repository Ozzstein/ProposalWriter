# LFP Cathode Active Material (LiFePO4)

## Overview
Lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4, LFP) is an olivine-structure cathode active material used in lithium-ion batteries. LFP offers superior thermal stability, long cycle life (>3,000 cycles), and low cost compared to NMC chemistries, making it the dominant chemistry for stationary energy storage (BESS) and increasingly for EV applications (BYD Blade, CATL LFP). Commercial LFP CAM is produced via solid-state synthesis (mixing of FePO4 + Li2CO3 precursors + carbon source → spray drying → calcination at 600-800C in inert atmosphere) or hydrothermal routes. The synthesis route critically determines product quality: D50 particle size, BET surface area, phase purity (olivine vs impurities), carbon coating uniformity, and electrochemical performance (discharge capacity, rate capability, cycle stability).

## Key findings
- China controls >99% of global LFP CAM production, with no EU production online as of late 2025 (source: [[sources/SRC-022]])
- Spray drying and calcination process parameters (temperature, atmosphere pO2, dwell time, carbon loading) are the primary determinants of final CAM quality (source: [[sources/SRC-012]])
- LFP solid-state synthesis involves multi-step kinetics with activation energies of 65-180 kJ/mol across decomposition, intermediate formation, olivine crystallization, and carbon coating steps (source: [[sources/SRC-011]])
- Electricity accounts for 39.7% of LFP battery manufacturing GWP, making clean electricity source the most impactful single decarbonization lever (source: [[sources/SRC-ED-015]])
- LFP CAM process energy is estimated at 3-6 kWh/kg based on NMC analog (Ahmed et al., 2017: ~4 kWh/kg NMC) (source: [[sources/SRC-ED-011]])

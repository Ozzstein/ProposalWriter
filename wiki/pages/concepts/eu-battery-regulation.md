# EU Regulatory Context: CRMA, NZIA, and Battery Regulation

## Overview
Three EU regulatory frameworks create the strategic and compliance context for the FAAM-ENI LFP CAM project. The Critical Raw Materials Act (CRMA, 2024) designates lithium and phosphate as strategic raw materials and mandates that the EU achieve domestic processing capacity for 40% of its annual strategic raw material needs by 2030 — directly requiring EU LFP CAM production. The Net-Zero Industry Act (NZIA, 2024) sets targets for EU manufacturing of net-zero technologies including battery cells and materials, creating demand-pull for domestic LFP CAM supply. The EU Battery Regulation (2023/1542, effective 2024-2027 in phases) introduces mandatory carbon footprint declarations (CFDs) for industrial and EV batteries (2025), maximum carbon footprint limits (2027+), and Battery Passport requirements — creating a compliance incentive for EU-manufactured LFP CAM with documented GHG footprint.

## Key findings
- CRMA 2024 mandates 40% domestic EU processing of strategic raw materials by 2030 — explicitly applies to LFP CAM as a lithium-containing strategic battery material (source: [[sources/SRC-023]])
- NZIA 2024 sets EU battery manufacturing strategic targets; LFP CAM is a priority technology class (source: [[sources/SRC-024]])
- EU Battery Regulation (2023/1542) requires carbon footprint declarations for industrial batteries from 2025 and maximum limits from 2027 — EU-manufactured electrified LFP CAM provides the lowest footprint path to compliance (source: [[sources/SRC-026]])
- Innovation Fund eligibility directly references CRMA/NZIA strategic autonomy objectives as qualifying criteria (source: [[sources/SRC-026]])

## Open questions

- How does the Battery Passport due diligence requirement apply to LFP CAM specifically — does it require primary LCA data (closing GAP-003 and GAP-004)?
- Will CRMA Article 14 domestic processing targets create procurement obligations that favor FAAM Brindisi LFP CAM over Chinese imports?
